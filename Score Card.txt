UOS Conduct, Accountability & Contribution Scorecard

> **Status:** Future Scope — not part of the currently frozen UOS V1 development  
> **User-facing name:** **My Standing & Contribution**  
> **Domain decision:** Use existing D01–D30 domains as a cross-domain module. Do not create D31 during V1.



1. Requirement

UOS shall provide a governed conduct scorecard for students, employees and other authorised institutional users.

The module must allow:

	●	complaint/report creation by scanning an RFID ID card, dynamic QR or searching institutional ID;
	●	student-to-employee, employee-to-student, student-to-student and employee-to-employee complaints;
	●	remarks, incident reports, formal cases, findings, sanctions and appeals;
	●	a relationship-specific Conduct Standing starting from 100/100;
	●	controlled score deductions only after an approved final finding;
	●	score restoration through successful appeal, approved improvement plans and verified institutional contribution tasks;
	●	separate Contribution Points, badges and optional gamification;
	●	governed automations and agents without automated guilt, sanction or score modification.



2. Scorecard Model

```text
My Standing & Contribution
Conduct Standing          88 / 100
Contribution Points       1,250
Standing Status           Improvement Plan Active
Active Cases              1
Pending Actions           2
Eligible Restoration      8 Points
Appeal Deadline           05 September 2026
Verified Service Hours    24
Badges                     6
```

2.1 Conduct Standing

	●	Default starting score: 100
	●	Allowed range: 0–100
	●	Maintained separately for each institutional relationship
	●	Cannot be edited directly
	●	Changes only through approved score-ledger entries

2.2 Contribution Points

Earned through verified positive activities such as:

	●	NSS/NCC/community service;
	●	event support;
	●	campus cleanliness and sustainability;
	●	mentoring or peer support;
	●	innovation challenges;
	●	sports/cultural support;
	●	emergency assistance;
	●	approved improvement or restorative tasks.

Contribution Points may exceed 100. They may drive badges, levels, certificates and optional rewards.

2.3 Standing Status

Examples:

	●	Good Standing
	●	Advisory Active
	●	Case Under Review
	●	Appeal Pending
	●	Improvement Plan Active
	●	Interim Restriction Active
	●	Suspended
	●	Cleared on Appeal
	●	Closed and Archived

Serious restrictions must remain visible separately from the numerical score.



3. Supported Users and Score Duration

Student

	●	Score starts when the student relationship becomes active.
	●	Continues throughout the course/student period.
	●	Does not reset on semester, department, hostel or campus transfer.
	●	Freezes at graduation, withdrawal or final exit.
	●	Readmission may create a new standing period while retaining previous history according to policy.

Employee

	●	Score starts when employment becomes active.
	●	Continues through transfer, promotion and role change.
	●	Freezes at separation.
	●	Rehire creates a new employment standing period while retaining historical records according to HR policy.

Temporary populations

Contractors, interns, vendor personnel and visitors may have relationship-period standing where required.

Multi-role person

A person may have separate standing records:

```text
Student Conduct Standing     92 / 100
Employee Conduct Standing    100 / 100
```

One relationship must not automatically affect another without an authorised cross-impact decision.



4. ID Card and ID Search Complaint Flow

Supported identification methods:

	●	RFID ID-card tap;
	●	dynamic QR scan;
	●	roll number;
	●	employee ID;
	●	institutional ID;
	●	name and department search;
	●	hostel room/bed context;
	●	transport/event manifest;
	●	unknown-person report for later identification.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Tap RFID / Scan QR / Search ID] --> B[Resolve Credential]
    B --> C[Resolve Person and Active Relationship]
    C --> D[Show Minimum Identity Preview]
    D --> E[Confirm Correct Person]
    E --> F[Choose Remark / Complaint / Incident / Appreciation]
    F --> G[Enter Category, Time, Location, Description and Evidence]
    G --> H[Submit]
    H --> I[Route to Correct Authority]
```

Minimum identity preview

Show only:

	●	photo;
	●	name;
	●	institutional ID;
	●	active relationship;
	●	department/programme/unit;
	●	active/inactive status.

Do not show:

	●	Conduct Standing;
	●	previous complaints;
	●	confidential case history;
	●	sanctions;
	●	personal contact information;
	●	unrelated academic, HR or financial information.

The report must store the resolved person_id, relationship_id, credential used, reporter, device/reader, date, time, location and verification method. The physical card is only an identification token; the case belongs to the person relationship.

6. Record Types

Remark / Advisory

A low-level observation or warning.

	●	No score deduction
	●	May require acknowledgement or clarification
	●	May expire after a configured period
	●	Repeated remarks may trigger human review
	●	Repeated remarks cannot automatically become guilt

Complaint / Incident Report

Captures reporter, subject, category, description, time, location, witnesses, evidence, urgency and confidentiality preference.

	●	No score deduction at submission
	●	Goes through triage
	●	Can be merged with duplicate reports without losing source history

Formal Conduct Case

Created only after authorised triage. It contains:

	●	allegation and policy version;
	●	investigator/committee;
	●	evidence and witnesses;
	●	notice and response;
	●	hearing/review;
	●	finding;
	●	sanction;
	●	score effect;
	●	improvement plan;
	●	appeal;
	●	closure/reopen.

Appreciation / Positive Contribution

Records verified positive behaviour and awards Contribution Points or recognition. It does not automatically erase serious misconduct.

8. Score Deduction

A complaint, remark or pending investigation must not reduce the score.

```text
Complaint Submitted             No Score Change
Case Under Investigation        No Score Change
Interim Safety Restriction      Status Flag Only
Approved Final Finding          Deduction Ledger Entry
Successful Appeal               Correction/Restoration Entry
```

Indicative severity model

|Outcome           |Indicative effect         |Restoration             |
|------------------|-------------------------:|------------------------|
|Advisory          |0                         |Not required            |
|Minor violation   |−1 to −5                  |May be fully restored   |
|Moderate violation|−6 to −15                 |Controlled restoration  |
|Major violation   |−16 to −30                |Committee-controlled    |
|Critical violation|Score plus formal sanction|No automatic restoration|

Actual values must be configurable and policy-versioned.

Score ledger fields

Every deduction or restoration must record:

	●	person and relationship;
	●	case and finding;
	●	policy/version;
	●	severity;
	●	points deducted/restored;
	●	approving authority;
	●	reason and evidence;
	●	effective date;
	●	appeal status;
	●	before and after score;
	●	audit reference.

No administrator or approver may directly overwrite the score balance.



9. Score Restoration

The score may increase through the following controlled routes.

Successful appeal

If a finding is overturned or modified, UOS posts a compensating correction entry. The original entry remains in history.

Approved improvement plan

Possible actions:

	●	awareness or policy training;
	●	counselling/behaviour support;
	●	restorative conference or apology where appropriate;
	●	repair or compensation;
	●	safety training;
	●	supervised institutional contribution;
	●	no-recurrence observation period;
	●	final review by the authorised authority.

Verified contribution tasks

```text
Current Conduct Standing                  88
Policy Awareness Programme Completed      +2
Verified Campus Service                   +4
No-Recurrence Period Completed            +4
Final Improvement Review                  +2
Final Conduct Standing                   100
```

Time-based rehabilitation

Eligible minor cases may support warning expiry, 30/60/90-day no-recurrence milestones, approved expungement or formal review after sustained improvement.

Restoration limits

|Severity|Rule                                                   |
|--------|-------------------------------------------------------|
|Minor   |Tasks and no-recurrence may restore all eligible points|
|Moderate|Tasks may restore part or all after review             |
|Major   |Committee approval required; no automatic restoration  |
|Critical|Appeal or formal rehabilitation review only            |

Conduct Standing cannot exceed 100.

	●	certificates;

Anti-gaming controls:

	●	no self-verification;
	●	no duplicate rewards;
	●	mandatory evidence;

	●	no public lowest-score leaderboard;
	●	serious misconduct cannot be cancelled by unrelated achievements.



11. Appeals for All Users

Every person affected by an adverse decision must have an applicable appeal or review route.

Eligible users may include:

	●	student;
	●	employee/faculty;
	●	contractor or temporary worker;
	●	authorised guardian for a minor;
	●	authorised representative;
	●	complainant seeking permitted review of closure or inadequate action;
	●	visitor or subject of an access restriction.

Appeal grounds

	●	mistaken identity;
	●	insufficient or false evidence;
	●	wrong policy or score calculation;
	●	procedural unfairness;
	●	conflict of interest or bias;
	●	disproportionate sanction;
	●	duplicate punishment;
	●	completed restoration not recognised;
	●	new evidence;
	●	retaliation.

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Draft
    Draft --> Submitted
    Submitted --> Admissibility_Review
    Admissibility_Review --> Accepted
    Admissibility_Review --> Late_Appeal_Review
    Admissibility_Review --> Rejected
    Late_Appeal_Review --> Accepted
    Accepted --> Independent_Review
    Independent_Review --> Upheld
    Independent_Review --> Modified
    Independent_Review --> Overturned
    Independent_Review --> Remanded
    Modified --> Score_Sanction_Updated
    Overturned --> Score_Sanction_Reversed
    Remanded --> Original_Case_Reopened
```

During appeal:

	●	the score entry may display Under Appeal;
	●	institution policy determines whether the deduction remains active;
	●	urgent safety restrictions may continue;
	●	the original ledger entry is never deleted.



12. Roles and Permissions

|Role                       |Main permission                                             |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
|Reporter                   |Submit remark, complaint, incident, evidence or appreciation|
|Subject/Respondent         |View permitted allegation, respond, submit evidence, appeal |
|Triage Officer             |Classify, merge duplicates, route or close with reason      |
|Investigator               |Collect evidence and conduct investigation                  |
|Conduct/HR Officer         |Manage authorised student or employee case                  |
|Protected Committee/Officer|Handle anti-ragging, POSH, safeguarding or protected routes |
|Decision Authority         |Approve finding and sanction                                |
|Appeal Authority           |Independently review appeal                                 |
|Mission Owner              |Create approved contribution mission                        |
|Verifier                   |Verify mission or improvement-task evidence                 |
|Ledger Service             |Post only approved score transactions                       |
|Auditor                    |Review permitted audit history                              |
|System Administrator       |Configure system; no case decision or direct score edit     |
|Agent/Automation           |Assist within approved tools and limits                     |

Separation of duties:

	●	reporter cannot decide the same case;
	●	investigator cannot decide the same appeal;
	●	task assignee cannot verify own completion;
	●	administrator cannot alter score directly;
	●	agent cannot approve its own recommendation.

15. Automation and Agents

Assist

	●	draft/translate complaint;
	●	convert voice input to structured report;
	●	explain policy, score change and appeal route;
	●	summarise authorised case history;
	●	explain restoration eligibility.

Recommend

	●	suggest correct routing;
	●	flag duplicate/related reports;
	●	identify missing evidence or conflicts;
	●	recommend suitable improvement tasks;
	●	flag SLA, fairness, retaliation or score anomalies.

Human decides.

Prepare

	●	complaint summary;
	●	case chronology;
	●	notice/hearing packet;
	●	appeal dossier;
	●	improvement plan;
	●	score-impact preview.

Human reviews.

Execute with approval

	●	create routed case;
	●	send approved notice;
	●	assign approved task;
	●	schedule approved hearing;
	●	post approved score adjustment/restoration;
	●	issue approved badge/certificate.

Autonomous low risk

	●	reminders;
	●	appeal-window alerts;
	●	stale-case escalation;
	●	no-recurrence milestone alert;
	●	pending-verification alert;
	●	duplicate-report warning.

Prohibited

Agents must never autonomously:

	●	determine guilt;
	●	dismiss protected complaints;
	●	deduct/restore score;
	●	decide appeal or sanction;
	●	disclose protected records;
	●	suspend, terminate or expel;
	●	affect salary, promotion, result or graduation;
	●	publicly rank low-scoring users.



16. Privacy, Fairness and Misuse Controls

Mandatory:

	●	complaint is not guilt;
	●	role/purpose-based access;
	●	protected complainant identity where applicable;
	●	anti-retaliation safeguards;
	●	conflict and recusal controls;
	●	field masking and complete access/export audit;
	●	duplicate and coordinated mass-report detection;
	●	false-report review without discouraging genuine complaints;
	●	no automated punishment;
	●	no hidden employee surveillance score;
	●	no public misconduct leaderboard;
	●	policy-versioned scoring;
	●	appeal-overturn and reviewer-bias monitoring;
	●	retention, correction, expungement and reopen controls.



17. Critical Scenarios

The implementation must handle:

	●	wrong, lost, borrowed or replaced RFID card;
	●	mistaken identity;
	●	person with multiple roles;
	●	anonymous or unknown-person complaint;
	●	duplicate/mass complaints;
	●	group incident with multiple subjects and witnesses;
	●	complaint and counter-complaint;
	●	retaliation;
	●	reporter/approver or investigator conflicts;
	●	complainant withdrawal;
	●	false evidence discovered later;
	●	subject refusing acknowledgement;
	●	late appeal;
	●	score policy changing while a case is open;
	●	student/employee transfer or exit with open case;
	●	readmission/rehire;
	●	partial task completion;
	●	rejected contribution evidence;
	●	offline reader/network failure;
	●	notification failure;
	●	incorrect agent recommendation;
	●	emergency restriction before final finding.
